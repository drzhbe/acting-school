ButterTapes
The Four Levels

What each level actually covers.

Progression here is ability-based, not time-based — you move when you can consistently demonstrate the criteria across multiple scenes, including unfamiliar material. Not just once.

Level 1
Foundation

Week 1 — the gap revealed

You film a cold self-tape before any teaching happens. Most people are surprised by how much is missing once they actually see themselves back — and that's exactly the point. You can't fix what you can't see.

What you'll cover

  • The handful of questions every actor needs to be able to answer about a scene — what your character wants, what would prove to them they got it, the moment everything turns, why the scene matters, and what old habit or belief your character has to let go of to move forward
  • Exercises that train you to genuinely listen and respond to your scene partner in the moment, instead of performing at them
  • Learning lines properly — word-perfect, quickly, able to say them a hundred different ways, and eventually without having to think about them at all
  • Getting a self-tape setup that actually looks professional

What "ready" looks like

You can consistently work out what's actually happening in a scene, even one you've never seen before. You know your lines cold — accurately, quickly, flexibly, and without conscious effort. Your self-tape setup works, and you can watch your own tape back and tell what's working and what isn't.

"You stop winging it."
Level 2
Activation

Week 1 — the gap revealed

A scene requiring a genuine shift in perspective, acted cold. This is where it becomes clear that knowing your Story Choices intellectually isn't the same as actually experiencing a scene from inside them.

What you'll cover

  • The Intermediate Story Choices — Moment Before (what your character was doing right before the scene starts), Inciting Incident, Dramatic Question, Dramatic Answer, End Tag
  • The Embodied Layer — the specific process for closing the gap between knowing a choice and genuinely experiencing it, through four core exercises
  • Doors and Activities — a structured exercise that layers in a foreign circumstance, environment, and relationship one at a time
  • Deepening everything from Level 1 until it's becoming instinctual

What "ready" looks like

You can identify all five Intermediate Story Choices consistently. Your Embodied Layer work produces genuine action, not just a feeling. You can calibrate for the right type and the right amount. Foundation material is becoming instinctual rather than effortful.

"You stop performing it and start living it."
Level 3
Innate

Week 1 — the gap revealed

Improvised talk-to-camera, no structure at all. This shows what's actually emerging naturally versus what's still being consciously constructed.

What you'll cover

  • Beats — breaking a scene down into its smaller sub-units, using the Beat Climax (the moment everyone in the scene shifts at once) as the marker
  • Writing your own scene — there's no scene pool at this level, you build the material yourself
  • Discovering your Actor Signature — the specific, repeatable quality that makes you castable and recognisable, found through two guided questions

What "ready" looks like

You can identify beats using beat climaxes as the marker. You've written your own scene, broken it down at scene and beat level, and applied all five process layers to it. Your Actor Signature is articulated in one clear phrase you can actually use as a creative constraint.

"You stop being a passenger in your own work."
Level 4
Inhabited

Week 1 — the gap revealed

A divided-character scene, played cold. This makes it clear that having a Want isn't enough on its own — there's more architecture underneath a genuinely complex character.

What you'll cover

  • Relationship — a specific method for working out how any person, place, object, or idea genuinely matters to your character's want
  • Character — the combination of a character's psychology and their physical reality, and how the two shape each other
  • Outdated Adaptation as inner conflict — the tension between the old strategy your character used to rely on and the new one the story forces them to adopt
  • Unified versus divided character work

What "ready" looks like

This is the graduation level. The marker isn't a technique — it's self-diagnosis. You can watch your own tape, identify what's actually not working (the domino), re-shoot, and improve independently. Feedback from Bede becomes confirmatory, not revelatory.

"The teacher is no longer necessary."
